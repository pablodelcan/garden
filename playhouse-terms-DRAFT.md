# Terms of sale — WORKING DRAFT, NOT LEGAL ADVICE

**Status: unreviewed draft. Do not publish as-is.** This was written by Claude, which is not a
lawyer. It exists so you can hand a marked-up starting point to an actual attorney rather than
paying one to stare at a blank page. Every clause below is a question, not an answer.

Product: printable plan sets from **Delcan & Co. Builder** (build.delcan.tools), sold pay-what-you-want
via Stripe. Structures are elevated children's playhouses.

---

## 1. What you are buying

You are buying a printable plan set: drawings, a cut list, a shopping list, and step-by-step
assembly notes generated from the design you configured. Delivery is immediate and digital.

## 2. This is a tool, not an engineering service

Delcan & Co. Builder is a design tool. It is not an engineering, architectural, surveying, or
construction service, and buying a plan set does not create a professional relationship of any kind.

The plan set is **not a stamped or signed engineering plan**. No licensed engineer has reviewed
your design. It has not been checked against your jurisdiction's building code, your site, or your
intended use.

## 3. What the structure check actually does

The tool compares members against published span tables at fixed assumed loads (currently 40 psf
floor live, 30 psf roof snow, 1,500 psf soil bearing, #2 SPF lumber). Those are assumptions, not
measurements of your site. It does not analyze wind, seismic loads, actual soil bearing, lumber
condition, connection capacity beyond the specified hardware, or workmanship. Passing the check
means the geometry falls inside those tables. It does not mean the structure is safe as built.

## 4. Your responsibilities

You are responsible for:

- confirming your local building code and obtaining any required permits and inspections;
- verifying the design suits your site, soil, climate, and intended use;
- engaging a licensed professional where the work calls for one, or where you are unsure;
- sourcing sound materials of the specified species, grade, and treatment;
- building safely and to a competent standard;
- supervising children and maintaining the structure over its life.

Quantities and costs are estimates. Verify them before ordering.

## Notes for the lawyer

Open questions I could not resolve and that seem material:

1. **Child injury.** These are elevated structures children climb. In many jurisdictions a parent
   cannot waive a minor's own claim. How much protection do clauses 5–7 realistically provide, and
   does anything else help?
2. **Design services.** Does generating structural specifications for money look like regulated
   design or engineering practice in any state where these will sell? Does it matter that the
   structures are small and non-habitable?
3. **Acceptance.** Terms currently live behind an "i" icon and on the printed sheet. To be
   enforceable they likely need affirmative acceptance at checkout. Stripe supports requiring
   agreement to terms — worth doing?
4. **Entity and insurance.** Recommendation was: form an LLC and carry general liability plus
   product liability / E&O before selling further. Please confirm the ordering and what coverage to
   ask for.
5. **The tool's own language.** The product makes affirmative statements about spans and structure.
   Section 3 above describes them accurately. Is there anything in the product UI that should be
   reworded further?
6. **Record-keeping.** Each design is encoded in a shareable URL, so the exact specification a buyer
   configured is reconstructable. Is retaining those useful or a liability?
